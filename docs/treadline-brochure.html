Grouped by rim, like the racks

Somebody counting stock walks one rim at a time. A sheet sorted any other way sends them back and forth all afternoon.

Used can be collapsed to a count

No brand, no model, no cost — a used tire can be collapsed so that it has none. One line: Size — Used · 6. Priced at the counter, where it's decided.

Oversells surface, they don't hide

You sold two you didn't have. The sale went through — the customer was standing there — and the row sits at the top until a manager reviews it.

Press a tire for its whole life

Every receive, sale, void and count, with the ticket number and who did it. "Where did the other six go" has an answer.

Three questions no notebook can answer

What's asleep? Sizes with your money in them and no sale in 45, 90 or 120 days. What should I order? Levels worked out from your own rate of sale — and editable, because you know things the numbers don't. What did I turn away? Every size a customer asked for that wasn't on the rack.

The customer is the headline

Every stage on this board is a version of somebody has to ring this person. The name and number come first; the ticket number is there, one size down.

Read the board by colour

Orange is a call the shop owes somebody. Green is waiting on the customer. Across a busy counter, that is the difference between glancing and reading.

Two answers, not "next"

A phone call has two outcomes. Both are on the card, worded as what happened — and the good one is the one that looks like the button.

Confirm, and undo

Every move names the customer before it happens, and can be taken back. Four near-identical cards is exactly where the wrong row gets pressed.

"Nobody ever called me" — answered with a date

How it got here opens the whole life of the order: ordered when, arrived when, who rang, and how many days it sat at each stage while somebody's customer waited. Read from the beginning, because it's a story.

Blind, and blind per tender

The expected figure stays covered until you've typed that tender's count. Type the card total and the cash figure stays hidden. The owner can skip the cover; nobody else can.

It stops the mistake happening

Try to cash in exactly the float first thing in the morning and it stops you: the drawer already carries the float — entering it here counts the same money twice.

One sheet to sign

The verdict, the reconciliation, the tips line and two signatures. That's the sheet that goes in the folder. Everything else prints behind it with a full report of your daily performance.

Catch the mistakes

Every tender is written down as it was rung, so a mistyped one shows up as a mismatch instead of hiding inside a net that happens to balance. What goes in the folder is the day's real figures, not the tidy version.

Why "covered" matters more than it sounds

A count you can see the answer to is not a count — it's a copy. Every shop that has ever been quietly skimmed was skimmed by somebody who could see the number they were supposed to hit before they counted.

Nothing is ever deleted

A void writes a reversing entry; it doesn't erase the original. A refund is its own row. The books can't be quietly rewritten — by anyone, including the owner.

Every change is signed

Who voided it, who discounted it, who moved the stock, who took cash out of the drawer — with a timestamp and a reason, permanently.

The alarm log remembers

Every exception the system has raised, who waved it away, when, and what they wrote. Dismissing an alarm doesn't silence it — it signs it.

Payroll weeks freeze

Once a week is paid it's locked. A correction made in August books into August, instead of quietly rewriting a July payout you've already handed out.

It tells you what's wrong, not just what happened

Most reporting hands you a chart and wishes you luck. Treadline reads your own numbers and writes the sentence: tire attachment fell nine points while the price per tire held flat — you're not losing customers, you're running out of stock.

09 — Time clock & timesheetsOptional add-on

Who was here, and for how long.

The same PIN that opens the register punches the clock. No second device, no second password, no card nobody can find on a Monday morning.

The wall screen says nothing

A keypad and the clock. Who is in, who is late and what anybody earns lives behind a sign-in, not on a sheet anyone can read from the waiting room.

One PIN, both jobs

Punch in at the kiosk, then open the register from the same screen without signing in twice. Walk away and it locks itself.

A forgotten punch-out is flagged, not guessed

The shift closes at nine and says so. Hours nobody confirmed are marked as assumed rather than quietly counted as worked.

Corrections are signed

A manager can fix a time, and the original stays underneath with who changed it and why. Payroll you can defend.

Clock out from the close-out

Whoever shuts the day sees who is still on the clock and can punch them out there — which is where somebody actually notices.
