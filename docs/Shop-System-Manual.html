01Signing in and out

  1. Tap your name in the list. Do not type anything yet.
  2. Type your four-digit PIN.
What just happened

The system now knows who you are. From this moment everything you do carries your name: every ticket, every payment, every correction. That is not there to watch you — it is what lets you prove you did not do something.

When you step away

Press Switch user at the top right. If nobody touches the machine for a while it locks itself.

Never lend your PIN

If somebody else uses your account, what they do is written down under your name, and there is no way to separate it afterwards. If you think somebody has learned it, ask for it to be changed the same day.

How to put two of the same tire on

  1. In Inventory mode, search the size and tap the tire. It is not added yet — it just loads, with its price.
  2. In the small box on the right — the how-many box — clear the 1 and type 2.
  3. Pick who fitted it.
  4. Press Add. Only now does it go on the ticket.
What just happened

The line reads 2 × with its price already multiplied. The Total below too. It is still one ticket with one line — that line just counts for two tires, and stock will take two off.

The quantity goes in BEFORE Add

That is the order, and it does not work the other way round from here: search, set how many, then Add. If it is already on with the wrong quantity, fix it from the ticket on the right by tapping the line and using How many.

And if they are two different sizes

The same: search the first, set how many, Add. Then search the second, set how many, Add. That leaves two lines on the same ticket. Nothing needs closing in between.

Never make two tickets for one car

It is the costliest mistake to unpick. Two tickets means two receipts, two payments and having the same conversation with the customer twice — and if one gets paid and the other does not, one of them sits in Pending for ever with nobody knowing why.

One car, one visit, one ticket. Even if it is four tires, mounting, balancing and a disposal.

08When they pay two ways

Example: the ticket is $300 and the customer gives $100 cash and the rest on a card.

  1. Press Split payment, or take only part of it.
  2. Pick Cash and type 100.
  3. Pick the second tender. The system has already filled in what is left.
  4. Press Take both charges.
What just happened

Two payments were recorded against the one ticket, and the ticket closed. On the night's count $100 shows under cash and $200 under card — which is exactly what will be in the drawer and on the terminal.

If they only pay part

The same, but enter only what they handed over and stop there. The ticket keeps a balance and appears under Pending on Main until the rest is paid.

09Closing without taking money

For when the work is written up outside and the customer pays indoors. Pressing that button brings up this box:

Create the ticket to pay at the cashier?

The ticket is finished at $300.00 with nothing collected — the customer pays at the cashier later. Any tires on it come off the racks now.

It sits in Pending on Main until the money is taken, and the total is frozen.

Go back
Create it

Read it. It is not there for decoration: it states the two things that happen the moment you press Create it.

  • The tires leave the racks now. Stock has already taken them off, even though nobody has paid.
  • The total freezes. The price can no longer be changed.
What just happened

The ticket closed with no money and went to the red box on Main, the one headed Pending. It stays there until somebody takes the money. If nobody does today, it will still be there tomorrow.

[[Go back]] breaks nothing

If you are not sure, press Go back. The ticket stays exactly as it was and nothing is recorded. The grey button is always the one that does nothing.

The two notes are not the same

  • Details → the job note. It prints on the customer's receipt.
  • Pin it → the yellow note. It does not print; it is for staff.
Mind which one you write in

Whatever goes in Details leaves with the customer on paper. If it is a message for a workmate — "would not pay today", "call his wife" — it goes in Pin it, never in Details.

Correcting a line already charged

Tap the line inside the drawer. Fix a price that was rung wrong appears there. It deletes nothing: it keeps the old price, the new one, who changed it and when, and the change appears on that night's close-out.

If the drawer is missing a button

It is your role, not the ticket. Voiding and correcting charged prices are a manager's. Ask for it — do not look for another way round.

Terminal taxWhat the terminal charges for paying tips out.

Why they are almost never the same

Because they measure different things, and the gap runs both ways:

  • Money came in that was not rung today: somebody came in to settle last week's ticket. That money is in Collected, but the work was another day's, so it is not in Sold.
  • Work was rung that brought no money today: a ticket that went to Pending, or one charged to an account. It is in Sold, but not a cent of it is in Collected.
  • Tips also land in Collected, and they are not the shop's sale.
The short rule

Sold = what we charged for working. Collected = what came into the till. To reconcile the night's money the one that matters is Collected, because that is the one you can count with your hands.

13The close-out

Done in Close-out, and the order matters.

  1. Count first. Take the money out of the drawer and count it. Type what you counted, and each terminal's figure.
  2. The system will not show you what it expects until you have typed. That is deliberate.
  3. Once you have typed, the difference column appears.
  4. Settle the card batch on the terminal and mark it here.
  5. Print and sign.
Why the count is blind

If the system showed you the expected figure first, counting would stop being counting and become looking for that number. Covered, the count is a real one. It is not distrust: it is the only way the figure means anything.

If it does not balance

DOES NOT BALANCE — out by $0.00 across tenders, netting to zero overall. Cash −$60.00 · Credit card +$60.00 — possible tender mix-up, see above.

That black box prints on the sheet. It says how much was over or short, and on which tender. When cash is short by exactly what card is over, it is nearly always one sale rung on the wrong button — and the system even names the tickets it might have been.

Never force the count

If money is missing, enter what you actually counted and print it that way. The sheet is a record of what happened, not of what anybody wanted. A tidied number looks fine that night and cannot be explained three weeks later.

El detalle

Sheet 1 — the one you sign

It looks the same every night. The day's figures — sold, tips, collected — the two week charts, staff performance, the count by tender, and the ruled signature lines at the foot.

Why the signature is always at the foot

So a missing page shows. If the signature comes out halfway down, or on the second sheet, something printed wrong — and that sheet is the one that gets filed.

Sheet 2 — Financial Report

The sheet of things to look at. It opens with the warnings — all of them together, at the top — then drawer movements, the midday counts, who went on account, who took what, and prices changed at the counter.

Sheet 3 — Logs Report

The detail. What is owed, what to reorder, the tires that went out, what arrived from the supplier, and every movement of the day in the order it happened.

All three are filed together

Every sheet carries the shop, the date and who printed it at the top, precisely because they get separated in the filing cabinet. If you find a loose page, it says which day it belongs to.

What just happened

Those tires are now on the rack as far as the system is concerned. If there were 2 and you entered 12, it now says 14 and they can be sold. It also recorded what they cost and who from, which is what lets the supplier's invoice be checked later.

Enter them the day they arrive

Enter them three days later and for three days the system said there were none — somebody may have told a customer we did not have them, or sold one against a no-stock warning. It is the easiest mistake to avoid and the hardest to trace back.

A tire the shop has never carried

If you search and it is not in the list, that brand or size is new to the system. Inside the same Receive stock panel, at the bottom, there is a small link:

  1. Press Not in the list? Add a tire.
  2. Enter the brand (say GOODYEAR), the model (TRAVEZA) and the size (195-65-15) in their separate boxes.
  3. Set the selling price.
  4. Carry on with quantity and cost as usual, and save.
Brand and model go in separate boxes

Do not type GOODYEAR TRAVEZA into one box. They go in two, which is why there are two. Typed together, the search stops finding them properly and the same tire ends up created twice under two different names.

Before creating one, search again

Search by size alone, without the brand. Very often it is there, just spelled differently. Creating one that already existed leaves two rows for the same tire, and from then on the count for that size is worthless.

Edit and Count are different

Edit changes what the tire is — its name, its size, its price. Count changes how many there are, and asks for a reason: miscount, damaged, sold off the books. If the number is wrong it is Count; if the name or price is wrong it is Edit.

Voiding is not refunding

  • Void = this ticket should never have existed. The money goes back and the tires return to the racks.
  • Refund = the sale did happen, but the customer brought the tire back later. The sale stays; the return is recorded separately.
The worst thing you can do

Trying to fix a mistake by ringing an opposite ticket, or overcharging elsewhere to make it balance. Everything has a proper correction and all of them leave a record. Ask before inventing one.

19Tips

A tip is recorded against the payment, not the ticket. That sounds like a detail and is not: it means a tip can be added later, even the next day, and it still reaches the right technician.

You forgot the tip

Do not ring another ticket. Open the original and add the tip to its payment. Never ring a tip as a sale: that puts money into the day's sales that was not a sale, and the count will not come out at night.

The terminal's charge is worked out automatically from the shop's rule. There is no arithmetic to do by hand and nothing to round.

21When the ordered tire arrives

When an order is marked delivered, a box opens and asks one question: was this tire already put into the stock count when it arrived?

It has to ask, because in the database the two look identical. A tire that arrived and was booked in with Receive stock is exactly the same row as a tire that arrived for an order. The computer cannot tell them apart. You can — you were holding it.

What the box shows you

  • Every tire on the ticket, with how many the count says are on hand right now.
  • Every movement that tire has had: when it came in, when it went out, who and on which ticket.
  • An amber tag reading added by hand on any receipt with no ticket against it. That one is very probably the one you are looking for.
  1. Tick only the tires that were already put into stock.
  2. Read the line at the bottom. It says in plain words what is about to happen.
  3. Tick I'm sure and press Fulfil the order.
The two wrong answers fail in opposite directions

Tick one that was not booked in and that size ends up one low. Fail to tick one that was and it ends up one high — which is worse, because the system will tell you there is a tire that is not there and somebody will promise it to a customer. Neither fixes itself. That is why there is an I'm sure tick.

If you are not sure, do not guess

Cancel, go and count that size on the rack, and come back. It costs two minutes. Guessing can cost weeks of a wrong number nobody can find.

23What Logs hides, and what it paints red

Logs is the list of everything that happened, row by row, in the order it happened. But not everything that happened is worth reading twice, and the screen sorts it into three.

The little eye

To the left of the Today button there is a little eye. When it has a line through it — which is how the screen always opens — the list is hiding the lines that were rung and struck off the same ticket.

That is not hiding anything: a line somebody typed wrong and corrected ten seconds later is not an event of the day — it is somebody using the register. On a busy afternoon they were half the list, and the rows that matter got lost among them. Press the eye and they all come back.

Voided tickets come out red

A voided ticket is a different thing entirely: it is a job that did not happen. Those are never hidden, and they now come out with the row tinted light red rather than merely struck through. When the drawer is short at night a void is the commonest explanation there is — the list has to show it to you from across the screen.

The Show voided tickbox is still there and still yours. The eye and that box are not the same switch: the eye is about single lines, the box is about whole tickets.

The owner can take a row off the list

Only the owner, and only on rows that are already dead — a voided ticket, a struck line, a payment that has been reversed. It is for clearing away test tickets and corrections that no longer tell anybody anything. It moves no money: if the row still carries live money the system refuses and says why. And nothing is destroyed — the owner still sees it in red with a deleted tag on it, and can put it back with one press.

24The customer book

From Logs, the Customers button opens the customer book: everybody who has ever left a name. The list is on the left, and pressing a name opens everything about them on the right.

  • Their cars — year, make, model, colour, plate and tire size.
  • Every ticket of theirs, newest first. Press one and it opens.
  • A pinned note, exactly like the one on Main: anybody writes it and everybody sees it.

The three tickboxes

BoxWhat it leaves on the list
Owes moneyOnly the people who still owe on a ticket.
Credit accountOnly the people running a tab with the shop, and of those only their account tickets.
Not approvedThe people running a tab that nobody approved for credit. That is the short list that matters.
The little star

A manager can mark a customer Credit approved from inside their card. From then on a star appears in front of their name on the list, and when they are picked on a new ticket a green box says Store Credit Approved. It is so the person at the counter does not have to go and ask.

A manager can correct the name, the phone and the cars, and can delete a car that has been sold on. Deleting the customer outright is the owner's only: their tickets keep their name, but they stop existing for anybody looking them up.

25House accounts

Some customers do not pay at the counter: it goes on the tab and they settle at the end of the month. That is rung against the Credit Account tender, and the ticket reads paid — the drawer balances at night, because as far as the till is concerned that money is no longer pending.

A house account is not an unpaid ticket

An unpaid ticket is settled at the register, sits in the box on Main, and is chased by whoever is at the counter. A house account is not: it builds up over weeks, is paid against the total rather than against any one ticket, and is handled by one person who is not on the floor. They are two different things and they do not mix.

Who can touch it

The owner and the accountant, and nobody else. A manager runs the floor and has no part in what somebody owes over a month.

Recording a payment

  1. Open the customer and press Record a payment.
  2. How much came in.
  3. When it was paid — not when you are typing it. A cheque dated the 3rd that reached the desk on the 11th is the 3rd's money.
  4. How — Cheque 4471, cash in person, card over the phone.
  5. To what account — Frost checking, the register.
If it went on the wrong customer

Press Show payments, find the row and press Void. It is not erased: its opposite is written. The wrong payment stays as the record that it was made, because the account it wrongly credited really did read low until somebody noticed, and a trail that erases that is worth less than no trail.

What went on the tab

Under the balance is the list of tickets that went on the tab. Charges that cancelled themselves out — a voided ticket, or a sale rung to the account and then corrected to the card the customer actually used — are hidden, because they are a charge and its opposite that net to nothing. The small show 2 cancelled link brings them back.

On paper they never appear, whichever way that link is left. A statement that hands the customer a $210 charge and a −$210 beside it has handed them arithmetic to do before they can read what they owe.

The statement

Print statement produces that customer's sheet: the shop's letterhead, their name, the balance, what went on and every payment. It comes out whole even when the payments are folded away on screen.

The whole month

The Accounts this month button, next to ← Logs, opens the month's sheet: one line per account, with four columns.

ColumnWhat it is
Owed at the 1stWhat they owed before the month started.
Went onWhat went onto the tab during the month.
Came inWhat they paid during the month.
Owed at the endWhat they owe now.
The four columns check each other

The first plus the second less the third must equal the fourth, on every line and on the total at the foot. That is why there are four and not one: a list of balances cannot be checked against anything. If it ever fails to add up the sheet says so in red, and it should not be filed until somebody has looked.

Below it is the list of every payment in the month — date, customer, how, to what account and who recorded it. At the top there is a month picker and a Print button.

26The month's tire sheets

From Logs, the Tire movements button opens the record of everything that has come in and out of the racks. At the top of that screen are two buttons that produce the two sheets asked for every month.

ButtonWhat it gives you
Sold this monthEvery new tire sold in the month, with the total and with the tax backed out.
Stocked this monthEvery new tire booked in, with what was paid for them and the tax backed out.

Both carry a pill on each row saying whether that tire was for a customer's order or off the rack, with a summary at the foot that counts them. Pressing a button turns it blue and folds away the long log underneath, so the sheet prints on its own. Press it again and it comes back.

The sheet is only as good as the costs typed in

The stocked sheet adds up the costs somebody typed when receiving. If a cost was entered wrong the total is wrong, and the sheet has no way to know. That is what the amber box above the summary is for.

27The Performance board on the close-out

On the Close-out, the line reading HOW THE WEEK IS GOING opens the two charts and the technicians' board. It comes shut on purpose: the close-out is a sequence — count, log, settle, sign — and neither of those is a step in it. They are for reading once the money is away.

Inside there is a picker with four ways to read it:

OptionCompares the work against…And the tips column shows…
DefaultLast week.Today's tips. This is exactly what prints.
By dayThe last day that technician was in, not yesterday.Today's against that day's.
By weekLast week.This week's against last week's.
By monthThe same days of last month.This month's against last month's.
Why **By day** does not compare against yesterday

Because not everybody is in every day. Measured against yesterday, anyone who was off shows red at −100% beside the people who were there — which says nothing about them except which days they had off. Each person is measured against their own last shift, which is the question that can actually be answered.

The picker does not change the paper

The printed sheet always comes out on Default — the week, with today's tips — whatever the picker says. A close-out is filed and compared against other close-outs: a column that said this month on one night and today on another would make the whole stack unreadable. And on paper that column answers what is going home in whose pocket tonight, which is not a weekly total.

28The wall board

The screen hung where the floor can see it. No PIN, refreshes itself every half minute, and there is nothing on it to press.

  • Top: who is ahead, in whichever category is selected.
  • Bottom left: today's tips.
  • Bottom right: the last tickets rung.
Why tips show as a share

Because customers can see that screen too, and a customer who learns what the technicians made in tips today may well decide to keep his five dollars. Show the dollars puts the amounts up for one minute and takes them down by itself — deliberately, because the failure of that button is pressing it and walking off.

A tipped ticket carries a gold edge and the amount in a gold badge, so it reads from the far side of the shop.

32The three rules

If nothing else here stays with you, keep these three.

  1. The grey button never does anything. When a box appears and you are not sure, press the grey one. You can always start again.
  2. Write down what actually happened. Never tidy a number to make it balance. The system copes with a mistake; it does not cope with a hidden one.
  3. Say so the same day. Almost everything is easy to correct today and a problem in a fortnight.

33Company accounts (fleets)

A company is a customer whose account several drivers charge to. Williams Insulation is a company; its drivers come in with company trucks and what they spend goes on the Williams account, not on their own. A driver with his own credit who works for nobody stays exactly as he is — none of this changes him.

The rule, in one line

If a customer is assigned to a company, everything they put on account belongs to the company. That is why there is nothing to choose when you take payment: it is chosen once, when the driver is set up. A driver who must be billed separately is simply not assigned to a company.

Step 1 · Mark the company

  1. Go to Customers and find the company — say Williams Insulation. If it does not exist yet (like Carrillo Construction), press New customer and create it first, under the company's name.
  2. With the company open, tick This is a company others can be billed to.
  3. Make sure Store credit approved is ticked as well. That is what says the shop carries that company.
What just happened

That company now appears in the Billed to list on every screen where a customer is set up. No balance has changed yet — it is simply ready to take drivers.

Step 2 · Add the driver

There are two ways in and they leave the same result. Use whichever is in front of you at the time.

  1. From the customer book. Customers → New customer. Type the driver's name and phone, and under Billed to pick the company. Press Add them.
  2. From a sale. Ring it as normal: put the driver's name and truck on the ticket, and when you choose Credit Account a blue box appears saying Billed to. Pick the company there. It is remembered for good — you do not do it again next month.
What just happened

The driver is now assigned to the company. From that moment every account charge of his adds to the company's balance — and the ticket still carries the driver's name and his truck, which is what later lets you tell the company exactly who spent what.

The truck comes with him

A vehicle captured on the driver's ticket also shows on the company's sheet. There is no second place to enter it and nothing to register separately.

Step 3 · Ring it as normal

There is no new step when taking payment. Put the driver on the ticket, choose Credit Account, and that is it. The charge goes to the company's account on its own.

The driver's balance is not his any more

If you look the driver up in Customers, his row says which company he is billed to and shows no separate balance — because he has none. The money lives on the company. If somebody asks "how much does this driver owe", the honest answer is how much he has put on the company's account, and that is on the company's sheet, line by line.

Step 4 · The company's sheet

  1. Customers → find the company → press Statement →.
  2. Pick the month at the top right and press Print.

The sheet carries four figures that reconcile — owed at the start, charged, paid, owed now — and under them every charge with its date, ticket, driver and truck. A charge rung on the company itself says on the account where a driver's name would be.

What the driver column is for

It is the difference between sending a number and sending a document. A company that receives "$4,200 for March" has to take it on trust; one that receives the list with driver, truck and ticket can check it against their own records. An argument about a charge becomes a five-minute lookup.

Things you will need

SituationWhat to do
The driver moved companiesOpen him in Customers and change Billed to. The past does NOT move: what he already charged to the old company stays theirs, because that is what happened.
The driver leaves and now pays himselfChange Billed to to Themselves, and tick his own Store credit approved if he is to be carried.
The company's owner comes in his own carIf his personal record is assigned to the company, that charge is the company's too. To keep them apart, make two customers: the person and the company.
The company paysTake it against the company's account, like any other payment. It brings down the balance for all its drivers at once, because it is one balance.
A company cannot sit inside another

The system refuses it, on purpose. One level — a company and its drivers — is what keeps a balance unambiguous. If it is genuinely needed, raise it before inventing a workaround.
